Cloud gaming

Cloud gaming streams games to your screen from powerful remote computers, so you do not need expensive hardware.

How it works

The game runs on a server in a data centre. Video is sent to your device, and your button presses are sent back. Almost any device with a screen, a controller and a good internet connection can play demanding games this way.

Services such as Xbox Cloud Gaming and GeForce Now work like this, though each has its own library and pricing.

The catch

Everything depends on your internet. You need a fast, stable connection with low delay, and wired or strong Wi-Fi works best. A weak or unstable connection causes blurry video and lag, which is a real problem for fast games.

Where connections are less consistent, cloud gaming can be hit and miss, so test a free trial before you commit.

What I look for

  • Connection speed and stability: low delay matters as much as raw speed.
  • Game library: check that your favourite games are available.
  • Subscription cost: compare it with buying hardware over time.
  • Device and controller support: phones, TVs and PCs vary.
  • Free trial: try it on your own network first.
