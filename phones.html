Phones

The phone is the one device almost everyone owns. It is our camera, wallet, map and entertainment centre in one slab of glass.

What makes a modern phone

A phone's value comes from how its parts work together: a fast chip, a bright display, a capable camera and software that keeps improving for years. Two phones with similar specs can feel very different in daily use, so I judge them by the experience, not just the numbers.

Today's phones also do more on the device itself, from photo processing to voice features and AI tools, which makes the chip and software support even more important.

The evolution of the phone

  1. 1983Motorola DynaTAC. The first commercially available handheld mobile phone. It was large, expensive and could only make calls.
  2. 1994IBM Simon. An early idea of a smartphone, with a touchscreen, email and apps, long before the idea caught on.
  3. 2007The iPhone. A full touchscreen and a real web browser changed what people expected from a phone.
  4. 2008Android and app stores. Android phones and app stores opened the platform to millions of developers and users.
  5. 2010sCameras and speed. Multiple cameras, faster mobile networks and all-day batteries became standard.
  6. TodayFoldables and on-device AI. Folding screens, 5G and smart features that run on the phone itself.

Phones in the Caribbean

Before you buy, check that a phone supports the network bands your local carrier uses, especially if you are importing it. Also check the warranty, because some warranties only cover the country of purchase.

What I look for

  • Software support: how many years of updates the maker promises.
  • Camera: consistent photos in everyday light, not just sample shots.
  • Battery and charging: a full day of real use and reasonable charging speed.
  • Display: brightness outdoors matters in a sunny climate.
  • Value: the best phone is not always the most expensive one.
