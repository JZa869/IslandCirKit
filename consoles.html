Consoles

A games console is a computer built for one job: playing games on your TV, with nothing to set up or upgrade. Here is how consoles went from bouncing dots to cinematic worlds.

What makes a console different

Every console of a given model has identical hardware, so developers can design games that run well on it. That is why consoles are simple: you plug in, pick a game and play. Many also offer exclusive games you cannot find elsewhere.

Games come on discs or as digital downloads, and many consoles offer paid subscriptions that include online play and a library of games.

The evolution of consoles

  1. 1972Magnavox Odyssey. The first home video game console, and it brought very simple games into the living room.
  2. 1977Atari 2600. Popularised cartridges, so one machine could play many different games.
  3. Mid-1980sCrash and revival. Poor-quality games crashed the North American market in 1983. Nintendo's NES revived it with stricter quality control.
  4. 1989 to 199116-bit and handhelds. The Sega Genesis and Super Nintendo competed, while the Game Boy made handheld gaming mainstream.
  5. 1994 to 19963D and discs. Sony's PlayStation brought disc-based 3D games to the mass market, and the Nintendo 64 added an analog stick.
  6. 2000 to 2001PlayStation 2 and Xbox. The PS2 became one of the best-selling consoles ever and played DVDs. Microsoft entered the market with the Xbox.
  7. 2005 to 2006Online and motion. The Xbox 360 made online play mainstream, and the Nintendo Wii used motion controls to reach new players.
  8. 2013PS4 and Xbox One. Digital stores, game updates and online features became part of the console experience.
  9. 2017Nintendo Switch. A hybrid that works as a home console and a handheld.
  10. 2020PS5 and Xbox Series X and S. Fast solid-state storage cut loading times, and ray tracing improved lighting.
  11. 2025Nintendo Switch 2. Nintendo followed its hybrid success with a new generation.

Choosing a console

  • Games you want: exclusives and your favourite series should decide it.
  • Where your friends play: online play is better with people you know.
  • Physical or digital: discs can be resold, downloads are more convenient.
  • Storage: modern games are large, so check space and expansion.
  • Your TV: look for 4K and 120 Hz support to get the most from newer consoles.
