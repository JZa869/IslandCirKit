Kitchen and laundry

Refrigerators, washers, dryers and ovens are getting Wi-Fi, apps and sensors. Some of it is genuinely useful, and some is a gimmick.

What smart appliances can do

Connected appliances can send a notification when a cycle finishes, let you start or pause a wash from your phone, or alert you if a refrigerator door is left open. Some track how much energy they use, and some can report faults so repairs are easier to diagnose.

Are they worth it

The best features are the practical ones: alerts, scheduling and energy information. Be cautious about paying extra for features you will not use. A smart appliance also depends on its app and servers, so ask how long the maker will keep supporting it.

The core job still matters most. A refrigerator should keep food cold, and a washer should clean clothes well, whether or not it connects to Wi-Fi.

What I look for

  • Core performance: cooling, cleaning and reliability come first.
  • Useful smart features: alerts, remote start and energy monitoring.
  • Energy efficiency: lower power use saves money every month.
  • Repairability: parts and service available locally.
  • Works without Wi-Fi: the appliance should still work if the connection drops.
