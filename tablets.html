Tablets

A tablet sits between a phone and a laptop. It is a large, light screen that is great for reading, drawing, watching and light work.

Where tablets shine

Tablets are comfortable for long reading sessions, video, note taking and sketching, and their battery life is usually excellent. With a keyboard case they can handle emails and documents, and with a stylus they become a digital notebook or drawing pad.

Their limit is serious multitasking. For heavy work like programming or video editing, a laptop is still easier.

How tablets developed

  1. 1990s to 2000sEarly attempts. Pen-based computers and Windows tablets existed, but they were heavy, costly and awkward to use.
  2. 2010The iPad. A fast touchscreen, a huge app library and simple design made tablets popular.
  3. 2010sAndroid and Windows options. More choices arrived, from budget models to laptop-style tablets.
  4. TodayLaptop alternatives. Powerful chips, keyboards and stylus support let many people use a tablet as their main computer.

Which type is right for you

For media and reading, a mid-range tablet with a good screen is plenty. For drawing or note taking, make sure it supports a quality stylus. For work, look at a larger screen with keyboard support.

What I look for

  • Screen quality: sharpness, brightness and size.
  • Stylus and keyboard support: the accessories that turn it into a work tool.
  • Storage: media and apps fill space quickly.
  • Wi-Fi or cellular: cellular costs more and is only needed if you use it away from Wi-Fi.
  • Software support: years of updates keep it secure.
